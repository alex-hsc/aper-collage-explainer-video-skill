# Art direction

## Contents

- Visual grammar

## Visual grammar

Use solid color fields, black-and-white halftone people or objects, colored cardstock, visible cut edges, cream outlines and soft paper shadows. Favor front-facing or slight three-quarter compositions and a locked camera. Build each shot from 3–6 large readable groups.

Default palette: deep purple background, warm red for gain/rise/attention, teal for loss/fall/counterpoint, cream outlines and charcoal halftone figures. Change the palette when the topic requires it, but keep semantic color roles stable across the video.

## Metaphor rules

- Convert an abstract claim into a physical action with a clear subject, object and direction.
- Use contrast, balance, containers, paths, growth/decay, assembling/disassembling and reveal/occlusion.
- Make the action readable in a still frame and without captions.
- Use one central action per shot. Remove props that do not explain the line.
- Avoid generic coins, charts, light bulbs, floating icons and text labels unless the narrative specifically depends on them.

## Continuity rules

Lock background texture, palette, paper thickness, outline color, shadow softness, halftone scale and character design in the style bible. Reuse reference images for recurring figures. Maintain consistent screen direction and semantic color meanings.

## Motion vocabulary

Prefer paper pieces that slide from outside frame, pop up, unfold, rotate slightly, stack, connect or reveal from behind another layer. Stage one group at a time in the declared assembly order. End on the approved composition and hold briefly for readability.

## Negative constraints

No camera cuts, zooms, parallax drift, photorealistic depth, liquid morphs, body deformation, extra fingers, new objects, fake text, captions, digits, logos, UI, watermarks or generated speech/audio.
